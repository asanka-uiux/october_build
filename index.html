The build story

A $12,000 truck.
One diesel show.

Custom Offsets Build Team . 5 Min Read

The brief was short: find a cheap truck, build something that can lay rubber and look right doing it, and have it on a trailer in eleven days, in time for FP Truck Fest 6.0.

We wanted to be into the truck for under ten thousand dollars, and we paid $12,000 on Facebook Marketplace. That difference is the Wisconsin rust tax, and anybody who has shopped for a twenty-year-old truck in this part of the country knows the number before they open the listing.

The truck

The truck is a 2006 GMC Sierra 1500 SLT with the VortecMAX 6.0 under the hood. The LQ9 is a cheap, stout, over-square V8 that takes boost without much convincing, and there are more of them sitting in salvage yards than almost anything else GM built; that matters when the budget is the whole point.

SLT trim is the reason a starlight headliner made the list at all. Strip a base truck and you are looking at a rubber floor and a bench seat; strip this one and there was something worth building back into.

Why a 6.0 going to a 6.0

The show is FP Truck Fest 6.0, run by Fitzgerald Performance on their own property on Commerce Drive in Jamestown, Tennessee, and the truck is a 6.0. Once that connection was made, the truck picked itself.

It also meant arriving at a diesel show with a gas truck, which was never a problem; it was the point. FP bills itself as the rowdiest diesel show in the country and has the numbers to support it: four hundred trucks in the judged Gold Lot out of more than twenty-five hundred applications, a Silver Lot that sold out in about two minutes, and a Chaos Pass capped at fifty vehicles for the entire event. Custom Offsets sponsors burnout alley, so we were going to be at this one either way. The only question was what we rolled in with.

What we changed

Forced induction was the headline, and it brought its own shopping list: larger injectors, a bigger fuel pump in the tank, and a tune to make the three of them agree. Mishimoto cooling went on because boost makes heat, and a 2006 half ton was never built expecting any of it.

The Starlight headliner took four straight days. The fiber was run into the headliner by hand, one strand at a time, with the truck gutted the entire time. We knew going in that it was the long job, so it started first and ran in the background while everything else went on around it. It is also the first thing anybody asks to sit in and look at.

The rest was stance and light: 26x14 ARKON Lincolns on 33x14.50 Venom Power Terra Hunter R/T Plus, leveling keys and upper control arms to make them sit the way they should, and Customs Unlimited lighting in the wheels, down the frame, and under the wheel arches.

Where the money went

The forced induction system is the headline at $6,549.00, which is more than half of what the truck cost. It took DeatschWerks injectors at $489.00, a bigger fuel pump at $1,129.00, and $1,682.00 in tune, plugs and fuel before it made a single clean pull.

The rest was spread thin and spent on looks, along with $425.95 for a B&M differential cover on the rear axle that nobody will ever notice at a show.

Getting it there

Powder coat is the one job you cannot recover by working later, so it went out early and came back when the shop was finished with it. The suspension waited on the powder coat, and the wheels waited on the suspension.

We were still working on the truck at 1am on the morning it had to leave. That is the honest version of a show deadline, and it is the one nobody puts in the build video. If you are doing this at home, start the slow jobs first. The headliner and the powder coat should have gone out on day one.

It is two days on a single ticket, with three vendor zones, a dirt track, a burnout pit, and sled pulls added for the first time this year with $20,000 going to the Hot Street winner. Because it is a 6.0 going to FP 6.0. Beyond that, a boosted VortecMAX makes the kind of noise and smoke that works in that crowd, and it does so for a fraction of what a built diesel costs to reach the same place. Nobody at the gate asked us to leave.

The truck cost $12,000 on Facebook Marketplace, and we had been trying to stay under ten. In Wisconsin, a clean twenty-year-old half ton with a 6.0 carries a rust-free tax, and that tax was the extra two thousand.

The Starlight headliner, by a wide margin. Four straight days of running fiber by hand with the truck gutted. We knew it was going to eat the clock, so we started it first and worked around it, which is the only reason it did not hold anything else up.
